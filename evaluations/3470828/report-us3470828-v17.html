Technology Analysis

Technology Profile — US 3,470,828

Plain-language description

US 3,470,828 describes a moving-ground-vehicle suspension system in which superconducting magnets carried by a train induce currents in longitudinal arrays of shorted conductive loops installed in a track bed. The magnetic interaction produces lift above a transitional speed and can also generate lateral restoring force, vertical stabilization, rotational stabilization, and damping of oscillatory motion. The patent also discloses alternative loop geometries and grouped diode-connected inductors for limiting induced current.

Innovation assessment

The central claimed combination is a moving vehicle carrying superconducting magnets interacting inductively with grounded, longitudinally arranged shorted loops to produce suspension and stabilization. The claims extend this architecture into distinct lift, lateral-stability, damping, geometric, and current-limiting configurations.

The invention combines known or separately searchable elements including superconducting magnets, conductive loops, inductive coupling, magnetic lift, passive restoring forces, damping circuits, and current-limiting inductors. The principal inventive-step question is not whether each component was known, but whether pre-filing evidence supplied a reason and reasonable expectation of success for this specific vehicle-track arrangement and its claimed stability/damping relationships.

  • Passive electrodynamic lift produced by vehicle superconducting magnets and non-superconducting track loops.
  • Geometry-dependent restoring force that stabilizes vehicle position without active feedback.
  • Integration of separate loop arrays for lift, lateral stabilization, and oscillation damping.
  • Grouped diode/inductor circuit arrangement for limiting induced track-loop currents.

These are analytical candidates, not established patentability findings.

The patent itself explores a broad design space: vertical loops, horizontal half-section loops, figure-eight loops, long damping loops, buried loops, road-level loops, alternative orientations, and grouped current-limiting circuits. It should therefore be evaluated as a design-space exploration event rather than only as one isolated embodiment.

  • Separate lift, guidance, stabilization, and damping loop arrays.
  • Linear motor or external propulsion systems combined with magnetic suspension.
