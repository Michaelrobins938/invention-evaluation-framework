## 3. Literature Findings

### State of the Art

The Utah Array (US5215088) remains the **gold standard** for intracortical neural interfacing:
- 20,000+ peer-reviewed citations
- Used in 1,000+ labs worldwide
- Only penetrating electrode array with FDA clearance for human use
- Used in humans since 2004
- 30,000+ aggregate days of human use
- Zero reported serious adverse device events
- Documented recording longevity exceeds 8 years in a single patient

### Key Technical Challenges Identified

1. **Chronic recording failure**: Short-term (acute) recording failure compromises long-term BCI function (Shen et al. 2015)
2. **Biocompatibility**: Glial scarring and tissue response around implanted electrodes
3. **Cable management**: Lateral tethering forces cause tissue damage (Lewis & Rosenfeld 2016)
4. **Scalability**: Current arrays limited to 96-128 electrodes; need 10,000+ for functional vision prosthesis

### Evolution Path in Literature

| Generation | Approach | Key Innovation |
|-----------|----------|----------------|
| Gen 1 (1989) | US5215088 | 3D silicon spire array |
| Gen 2 (1990s) | Utah Slanted Electrode Array | Graded electrode lengths for peripheral nerves |
| Gen 3 (2000s) | NeuroPort Array | FDA-cleared for human use |
| Gen 4 (2020s) | Neuralace | Flexible, 10,000+ channels |
