# Novelty Search Report — US5215088

## 1. Claim Construction (Provisional)

**Note**: This is an analytical construct, not a legal claim construction. Based on the patent's claim language and technology profile.

**Claim 1 Core Elements**:
1. A three-dimensional electrode device for placing electrodes in close proximity to cells lying at least about 1000 microns below a tissue surface
2. A base of rigid material
3. A plurality of elongated tapered electrodes of a first material mounted to said base to extend away from said base
4. Said electrodes being electrically isolated from each other at said base by means of a second material positioned between said electrodes
5. Each of said electrodes being at least about 1000 microns in length and having a distal end
6. Signal connection means linked with said electrodes for providing electrical connection to each of said electrodes individually

## 3. Prior Art References Identified

### Reference 1: Najafi & Wise (1985) — HIGHLY RELEVANT

**Citation**: K. Najafi, K.D. Wise, T. Mochizuki, "A high-yield IC-compatible multichannel recording array," IEEE Transactions on Electron Devices, Vol. 32, No. 7, July 1985, pp. 1250-1254.

**Publication date**: July 1985 (4 years BEFORE US5215088 filing)

**Description**: A multielectrode recording array for neural recording. Uses silicon substrate with deep boron diffusion and anisotropic etch stop. Supports array of polysilicon or tantalum thin-film conductors insulated with silicon nitride and silicon dioxide. Probe dimensions: 3mm length, 50μm shank width, 15μm thickness. Compatible with on-chip MOS circuitry for signal amplification and multiplexing. Complete ten-channel signal processor requiring only three external leads.

**Relationship to US5215088**:
- **Similarities**: Silicon-based electrode array; on-chip multiplexing; neural recording application
- **Differences**: 2D (planar) structure, not 3D; thin-film conductors on substrate, not penetrating pillars; no tapered/spire shape; no thermomigration/glass melt isolation; no 1000μm+ length requirement

**Structural classification**: E2 (shared components — silicon substrate, multiplexing)

### Reference 2: Edell et al. (1986) — RELATED

**Citation**: D.J. Edell, V.M. McNeil, L.D. Clark, "Microfabrication technology for development of chronic neural information transducers," Proc. Internat. Electron Dev. Meet., 1986, pp. 180-183.

**Publication date**: 1986 (3 years BEFORE US5215088 filing)

**Description**: Microfabrication techniques for chronic neural transducers. Referenced in US5215088 as background art.

**Structural classification**: E1 (same domain — neural transducers)

### Reference 3: Campbell et al. (1991) — SELF-PRIOR-ART (post-filing)

**Citation**: P.K. Campbell, K.E. Jones, R.J. Huber, K.W. Horch, R.A. Normann, "A silicon-based, three-dimensional neural interface: manufacturing processes for an intracortical electrode array," IEEE Trans Biomed Eng, 38(8):758-768, 1991.

**Publication date**: August 1991 (AFTER US5215088 filing of Nov 7, 1989, but BEFORE grant of June 1, 1993)

**Description**: Describes the exact fabrication process for the Utah Array: thermomigration of aluminum pads through n-type silicon block, mechanical/chemical micromachining, metal deposition, polyimide encapsulation. 100 electrodes, 1.5mm length, 0.09mm base thickness.

**Relationship to US5215088**:
- This is the INVENTOR'S OWN paper describing the same invention
- Published after filing but before grant
- Can be used as prior art against the patent (self-prior-art)

**Structural classification**: E5 (same mechanism — identical device)

### Reference 4: Brindley & Lewin (1968) — BACKGROUND

**Citation**: G.S. Brindley, W.S. Lewin, "The sensations produced by electrical stimulation of the visual cortex," J. Physiol., 196, pp. 479-493, 1968.

**Description**: First demonstration of phosphene perception from cortical stimulation. Surface electrodes, not penetrating.

**Structural classification**: E3 (same functional objective — vision prosthesis)

### Reference 5: Dobelle & Mladejovsky (1974) — BACKGROUND

**Citation**: W.H. Dobelle, M.G. Mladejovsky, "Phosphenes produced by electrical stimulation of human occipital cortex, and their application to the development of a prosthesis for the blind," J. Physiol., 243, pp. 553-576, 1974.

**Description**: Surface electrode arrays for visual cortex stimulation. Not penetrating.

**Structural classification**: E3 (same functional objective — vision prosthesis)

### Reference 6: Wise et al. (1975) — BACKGROUND

**Citation**: K.D. Wise, J.B. Angell, "A Low-Capacitance Multielectrode Probe for Use in Extracellular Neurophysiology," IEEE Trans. Biomed. Eng., BME-22(3):212-219, 1975.

**Description**: Early silicon-based neural probe. 2D planar design.

**Structural classification**: E2 (shared components — silicon electrode)

## 6. Self-Prior-ART Exposure

US5215088's inventors (Normann, Campbell, Jones) published Campbell et al. (1991) describing the same device. This creates:

- **Anticipation exposure**: LOW — the paper does not fully describe integrated multiplexing
- **Obviousness exposure**: MODERATE — the paper demonstrates the combination was achieved by the inventors themselves
- **Double-patenting exposure**: Possible if continuation patents claim the same subject matter

## 7. Combination-Obviousness Exposure (Derivation Risk)

The invention combines known elements:
- Silicon electrode arrays (Najafi & Wise 1985)
- Multiplexing circuitry (Najafi & Wise 1985)
- 3D penetrating architecture (not in prior art)
- Thermomigration isolation (from semiconductor processing, not neural interfaces)

**Derivation risk**: MODERATE. The 3D penetrating architecture with thermomigration isolation appears to be a novel combination. However, the motivation to move from 2D to 3D was clear (lower currents, better neuron access), and the fabrication techniques were available.
