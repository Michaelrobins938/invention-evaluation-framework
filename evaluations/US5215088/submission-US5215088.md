**Title and technical field**:
- Title: THREE-DIMENSIONAL ELECTRODE DEVICE
- Technical field: Three-dimensional semiconductor electrode arrays, neural interfaces, cortical implants, vision prosthesis, neuron interface devices
- Classification: A61B 5/04; U.S. Cl. 128/642; 128/784

**Short description**: A three-dimensional electrode device useful as a neuron interface or cortical implant. A plurality of spire-shaped electrodes formed of semiconductor material are associated with a rigid base, electrically isolated from each other at the base. Multiplexing circuitry allows the electrodes to be addressed individually.

**Detailed description**: The device comprises a rigid base of semiconductor material (preferably silicon) with a plurality of elongated, tapered (spire-shaped) electrodes extending from the base. The electrodes are at least about 1000 microns in length (preferably ~1500 microns) to penetrate cortical tissue and reach neurons ~1500 microns below the surface. The electrodes are electrically isolated from each other at the base by either:
1. Thermomigration process: Aluminum-doped silicon columns created via thermomigration through a silicon block, forming p-n junctions between adjacent columns.
2. Glass melt process: Channels of glass melted between silicon pillars to provide electrical isolation.

Multiplexing circuitry (AND gates, X/Y shift registers) on the back side of the base allows individual addressing of each electrode with minimal lead wires (only 5 wires needed). The electrode tips include charge transfer material (platinum, iridium, or iridium oxide) for transducing electrical charge to ionic charge in neural tissue. Two insertion mechanisms are disclosed: pneumatic impact inserter and mechanical spring inserter.

**Key technical features**:
- Three-dimensional spire-shaped semiconductor electrodes (≥1000 microns length)
- Rigid silicon base with electrical isolation between electrodes
- Multiplexing circuitry for individual electrode addressing
- Charge transfer material at distal ends (Pt, Ir, IrOx)
- Two fabrication methods: thermomigration and glass melt isolation
- Pneumatic and mechanical impact insertion mechanisms
- Passivation with polyimide or silicon nitride/silicon dioxide

**Background / related research**:
- Prior art includes 2D electrode arrays on cortex surface requiring higher currents (~3 mA) causing pathogenic problems
- 2D arrays experience electrode interaction producing anomalous phosphene positions
- Limited number of contiguous phosphenes achievable with prior implants
- Prior approaches required individual wire connections to each electrode (cumbersome)

**Innovation claims stated in the patent**:
- **Claim 1 (core combination)**: A three-dimensional electrode device for placing electrodes in close proximity to cells lying at least about 1000 microns below a tissue surface, comprising: (a) a base of rigid material; (b) a plurality of elongated tapered electrodes of a first material mounted to the base, electrically isolated from each other at the base by a second material, each electrode at least about 1000 microns in length with a distal end; and (c) signal connection means linked with the electrodes for providing electrical connection to each electrode individually.
- **Dependent features**: Electrical gates associated with each electrode (claim 2); multiplexed gates (claim 3); gates on opposite side of base (claim 4); charge transfer material at distal ends (claim 5); semiconductor first material (claim 6); semiconductor doped with impurity for p-n junctions (claim 7); glass second material (claim 8).
- **Claim 9 (neuron interface)**: A neuron interface device for placing electrode tips in close proximity to cells lying at least about 1000 microns below a tissue surface, with semiconductor electrodes having parallel longitudinal axes, tapered toward distal ends, with electrical connection means.
- **Manufacturing methods**: Thermomigration process; glass melt process; dicing saw for 3D structure formation; swirl etch and static etch for spire shaping; metal deposition for charge transfer; passivation with polyimide or silicon nitride.
- **Design objective**: Three-dimensional cortical implant for vision prosthesis that penetrates to neuron depth (~1500 microns), reducing current requirements and electrode interaction compared to 2D surface arrays.
