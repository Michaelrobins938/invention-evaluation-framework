## 1. Idea description
Nano-scaled graphene plates (NGPs) are nanometer-scale platelets of graphite consisting of one or few graphene sheets with at least one dimension ≤100 nm, produced by carbonizing a polymer or pitch precursor, exfoliating graphite crystallites, and mechanically attriting to nanoscale. The material is positioned as a low-cost alternative to carbon nanotubes for reinforcement and functional fillers.

## 3. Feature-benefit map
| Feature | User-facing benefit |
|---|---|
| Plate thickness <100 nm, preferably <10 nm | Higher specific surface area for interfacial load transfer in composites |
| Lateral dimensions <100 nm | Improved dispersion in resins, reduced agglomeration |
| Few-layer stacks <20 layers | Retains graphene-like mechanical/electrical properties while processable |
| Produced from low-cost polymer/pitch precursors | Cost advantage vs carbon nanotubes |
| Exfoliation + mechanical attrition process | Scalable, tunable plate size via HTT and milling time |
| Compatible with polymer/ceramic/metal/glass matrices | Broad formulation flexibility for composites |

## 4. Innovation assessment
- What differs from known approaches: Prior art focused on carbon nanotubes via arc discharge/CVD with low yield and high cost. This invention uses polymeric carbon derived from cheap precursors, exfoliates graphite crystallites, then attrites to nanoscale plates, claiming cost-effective mass production.
- Combination-obviousness exposure: Process combines known steps — carbonization of polymers, acid intercalation/exfoliation of graphite, ball milling — to produce nanoparticles. Derivation risk: each step is known; novel combination is specific sequence to produce nanoscale graphene plates from polymeric carbon. No unexpected result confirmed from record alone.
- Specific technical elements claimed as unique: Production of nanometer-scaled platelets where length/width/thickness ≤100 nm from polymeric carbon via exfoliation + attrition; specific plate size control via HTT, intercalation, milling.
- Design-space position: Appears to be early exploration of nano carbon platelets as CNT substitutes; limited sibling filings visible from source.
