## 3. Description of the invention

A combination hammer and lumber manipulating tool for providing a hammer which is
capable of being utilized as a lumber manipulating tool for use in combination with
an air nailer. The device includes a handle, and a hammer-head having a claw, a
socket and a striking head. The hammer-head further includes a jaw attached to a
side of the socket distally spaced in opposition to the claw. The distal space
between the claw and the jaw is formed to fittingly engage a piece of lumber for
manipulating. The jaw preferably includes a plurality of teeth for gripping the
piece of lumber. In an alternative embodiment, the jaw is secured to the handle
adjacent the claw (one-piece hammer). In use, when a piece of lumber is twisted
(e.g., due to water damage), the user positions the jaw and the claw around a
portion of the lumber's cross section and manipulates the handle towards the claw,
forcing the lumber to twist into the desired position, after which the user air
nails the lumber to retain it in position.

## 4. Background / problem being solved

When using an air nailer to force nails into lumber, a piece of lumber frequently
becomes twisted, requiring the user to manipulate the twisted lumber straight
again. Prior practice forced the user to twist the lumber by hand or use a tool
other than a hammer. Prior combination hammer-and-wrench devices (Maggard 711,408;
Mathews 513,271; Boswell 1,308,694) disclosed hammers with **movable** jaws that
slide and lock, which the specification criticizes as adding weight, adding moving
parts that wear and cause uneven impact, and not being designed for engaging lumber.
Thomas 4,762,303 disclosed a dedicated lumber-turning tool (elongated handle with
two opposed claws) that the specification criticizes as requiring the user to carry
a heavy elongated bar in addition to a hammer and air nailer.

## 5. Innovation claims

Thirteen claims; two independent claims:

- **Claim 1 (primary):** A combination hammer and lumber manipulating tool
  comprising: a hammer-head having a claw, a striking head and a socket for
  receiving a handle; and a jaw **non-movably secured to said socket** opposing
  said claw and spaced from said claw a finite distance, wherein said piece of
  lumber is positionable between said jaw and said claw.
- **Claim 6 (independent):** A combination hammer and lumber manipulating tool
  comprising: a hammer having a hammer-head and a handle attached to said
  hammer-head; said hammer-head includes a claw and a striking head; and a jaw
  **non-movably secured to said handle** opposing said claw and spaced from said
  claw a finite distance, wherein said piece of lumber is positionable between
  said jaw and said claw.
- Claims 2–5 depend on claim 1 (jaw taper, teeth, finite distance ≈ lumber width,
  weight ≈ conventional hammer-head).
- Claims 7–13 depend on claim 6 (jaw taper, teeth, finite distance, weight, jaw
  angled upwardly, one tooth at distal end).

The distinguishing feature over the movable-jaw prior art is the **non-movably
secured (fixed) jaw** opposing the claw, with no moving parts and approximately
conventional hammer weight.
