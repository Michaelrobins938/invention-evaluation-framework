## Invention Submission — US 8,527,057 B2

**Title:** Retinal Prosthesis and Method of Manufacturing a Retinal Prosthesis

### Inventors, assignee, and dates

- Inventors: Robert J. Greenberg; Jerry Ok; Jordan Neysmith; Kevin Wilkins; Neil Hamilton Talbot; Da-Yu Chang.
- Assignee: Second Sight Medical Products, Inc., Sylmar, California.
- Provisional application: US 60/675,980, filed April 28, 2005.
- Parent application: US 11/385,315, filed March 20, 2006; later issued as US 7,881,799.
- Divisional application: US 12/976,650, filed December 22, 2010.
- Prior publication: US 2011/0118807 A1, published May 19, 2011.
- Patent grant: US 8,527,057 B2, September 3, 2013.
- Submission date: 2026-08-15.

### Technical field and description

The patent concerns implantable neural stimulation, retinal prostheses, hermetic
packaging, inductive power/data coupling, and epiretinal electrode arrays.

It describes an implantable retinal prosthesis in which a retinal electrode array,
hermetic electronics package, and secondary inductive receiving coil are mechanically
arranged on a strap surrounding the sclera. The electronics package uses a ceramic
substrate with conductive vias, a flip-chip ASIC, wire-bonded passive components, and
a laser-welded metal lid; the package and coil are arranged to reduce implant height.

The implant includes an epiretinal flexible electrode array connected by a cable through
the sclera to an electronics package. A secondary inductive coil receives power and
data from an external primary coil. A molded silicone body holds the package and coil
in an end-to-end or alternative surrounding arrangement and narrows into a strap with
suture tabs and, in an embodiment, a surgical-tool hook.

The hermetic package includes a ceramic substrate, metallized conductive vias,
thin-film metallization, a flip-chip ASIC, a hybrid substrate carrying passive
components, gold wire bonds, a brazed metal case wall, and a laser-welded metal lid.

### Innovation claims

- Claim 1 combines an electrode array, sclera-surrounding strap, hermetic package
  containing a flip-chip circuit, electrical cable, and strap-mounted coplanar
  secondary inductive coil powering the circuit.
- Dependent features include suture tabs, a fan tail, surgical-tool hook,
  polymer-sandwiched metal-trace cable, cable folding, wound-wire coil, and oval coil.
- Manufacturing disclosure includes ceramic vias, flip-chip bonding, wire-bonded
  passive stack, brazed case wall, vacuum bake-out, optional getter, and laser-welded lid.

# 2. Technology Profile

## Plain-language description

An implantable epiretinal prosthesis combines a retinal electrode array with a
hermetic ASIC package and inductive receiving coil mounted on a scleral strap. Ceramic
vias, flip-chip and wire-bond interconnects, a brazed case wall, vacuum bake-out, and a
laser-welded lid create a compact implant intended to reduce tissue irritation and
obstruction of eye movement.

| Coplanar package and coil | Reduced cumulative implant height | US8527057B2 abstract/specification |

| Brazed wall and laser-welded lid | Protection against moisture/body fluids | Specification |

## Innovation assessment

The claimed delta is the integrated low-profile mechanical arrangement and hermetic
package architecture, not retinal stimulation by itself. Known elements include retinal
electrodes, inductive coupling, ceramic feedthroughs, flip-chip packaging, and implant
fixation. This creates combination-obviousness exposure. The specific unique elements
are the strap-mounted package and coil, coplanar low-profile arrangement, and mixed
flip-chip/wire-bonded hermetic package.

## Regulatory and development assessment

FDA's device-regulation overview identifies classification, registration/listing,
premarket pathways, IDE requirements, QMSR/21 CFR Part 820, labeling, and medical-device
reporting. Scope-level regulatory burden is **High**; product-specific classification
and pathway remain unestablished.

Development stage: patent-disclosed device architecture. Prototype, validation,
clinical, production, and sales evidence remain unestablished. Next milestones include
hermeticity, fatigue, ocular-motion, biocompatibility, electrical bench, animal/clinical,
and regulatory-pathway testing.

fixation. The claimed state integrates those components into a low-profile scleral strap
with coplanar package/coil geometry and a specified manufacturing stack. The required
change is that coordinated integration. Analogous motivation is present; direct
motivation and an unexpected technical result are not established. The bridge is
recorded as **TRAVERSED for analytical testing**, with a partially grounded motivation
and an evidentiary barrier against a stronger legal conclusion.

Per-gate assessment: utility has architectural support; novelty is not established as
a final conclusion; inventive-step evidence is only partially grounded. This is not an
